Money Around the World

Look in your pocket. What is inside? Coins? Notes? This is money, and every country calls its money by a different name. We call the name of a country's money its currency.

In the USA, people use dollars ($). One dollar is 100 cents.
In the United Kingdom, people use pounds (£). A small coin there is called a penny.
In the European Union, many countries share one currency — the euro (€).
In Russia, people use rubles (₽).

Money comes in two forms: coins for small amounts, and notes (paper money) for bigger amounts.

When you go shopping, you can ask: "How much does it cost?" The answer might be "It costs 5 dollars" or "It costs 2 pounds."

⚠️ Be careful! 105 euros is NOT the same as 105 rubles. Always check the currency symbol before you shop — or you might be in for a surprise, just like Peter the raccoon was!
